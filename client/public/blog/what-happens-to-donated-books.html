What Happens to Donated Books? A Local Pickup Service Explains

Last Mile Books · August 13, 2026 · A behind-the-scenes look at book donation

When you donate books to Last Mile Books, every book is sorted by hand. Books that can be resold or passed along to a new reader are prepared for that. Books that cannot be reused are recycled responsibly. Here's the full step-by-step of what actually happens.

Step 1 — Pickup at your door

A pickup starts with a request through our website or a phone call. Within one business day, we confirm a date and window with you. On the scheduled day, we drive to your address in Temecula, Murrieta, Menifee, or wherever you are in Riverside County, and load every box into our vehicle. You don't lift anything.

Step 2 — Sort by hand

Every box that comes in gets opened. Every single book gets looked at. This is where the real work happens — a book that looks like a beat-up paperback might turn out to be a first-edition worth passing to a specific reader. A cookbook that looks ordinary might be exactly what someone learning Italian cooking is searching for right now.

What we look at during sorting

  • Condition: Is it clean, dry, and intact? No mold, no water damage, no missing pages.
  • Age and edition: Is this a recent edition of a textbook? A first edition of a classic? A vintage cookbook?
  • Subject and demand: Does someone want to read this right now? Local libraries, schools, and readers all have different needs.
  • Physical properties: Hardcovers hold up better in resale, paperbacks are easier to pass along locally.

Step 3 — Find a new reader

Books that can find a new reader go one of a few directions:

  • Direct-to-reader marketplaces — books that have a specific reader searching for them get listed for that reader. This funds the free pickup service.
  • Local community — some books go to Little Free Libraries, community centers, and neighbors who ask.
  • Schools and literacy programs — children's books and educational materials go where kids will actually read them.

Step 4 — Responsible recycling for what can't be reused

Not every book can find a new reader. Water-damaged books, moldy books, encyclopedia sets, and books that are just too far gone go into responsible paper recycling. That means they go to a paper mill and become new paper products.

Why hand-sorting matters

Large chain donation centers process books in bulk. A big percentage of donated books at those places get recycled or shredded without anyone looking at them — the sorting cost is too high for the value they get out of it. Because we only handle books, we can spend the time.

That's the reason we exist. A book that took someone hours to read, thought about, marked up, and shelved — that book deserves someone to look at it before deciding its fate.

What you can do to help the book find a new reader

  • Keep the books dry. Don't leave them in a damp garage for months before donating.
  • Box them if you can. Boxes make sorting faster and protect the books in transit.
  • Include everything. Even books you think aren't valuable — you'd be surprised what someone is searching for.
  • Tell us about anything special. Signed books, family heirlooms, notable editions — mention them when scheduling and we'll take extra care.
